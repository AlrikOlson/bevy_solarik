# Lighting notes

These are the implementation details behind the changes from `bevy_solari` 0.19.1. See the [README](../README.md) for setup.

**A sky.** Upstream lights a scene from directional lights and emissive meshes only; a ray that leaves the scene contributes nothing, so anything lit by the sky alone renders black. Here a `SolarikSkyLight` resource (a cubemap in the scene's radiance units, plus an intensity) is bound into the ray-traced scene and read wherever a traced ray escapes: the ReSTIR GI first bounce (the escaped ray becomes a far sample point and is reused like any other), the world cache's GI rays (traced to the end of the world so distant geometry is not mistaken for sky), the specular GI paths and the pathtracer.

The sky is not in the direct-light list on purpose. A first version put it there as uniform-sphere light samples; in a street canyon almost every sample that wins the resampling is occluded, and the spiky estimate lost about two stops through the tonemapper and the denoiser. Rays that escape are visible by construction, so the miss paths are the better estimator. The first realtime GI bounce now mixes cubemap importance sampling with a cosine hemisphere; both traced hits and misses use the reciprocal mixture PDF. Below-surface sky draws remain counted zero samples. See [sky sampling measurements](sky-sampling.md) for the two-pass CDF, GPU validation and the limits of the measured result.

**Alpha-masked and blended materials.** Upstream builds every BLAS opaque and the rays see leaf cards and window glass as solid quads. Here the binder looks at the materials on each mesh: a mesh with a `Mask` or `Blend` material gets its BLAS rebuilt without the `OPAQUE` flag, and `trace_ray` walks the candidate hits (`rayQueryProceed` / `rayQueryConfirmIntersection`), alpha-testing `Mask` candidates against the base colour texture and never confirming `Blend` ones (glass is transparent to light). Double-sided materials hit from behind are shaded from the side the ray arrived on, as the rasteriser already does. The material flags live in what used to be `GpuMaterial`'s padding, so the layout is unchanged. `SolarikAlphaTesting(false)` turns it all off for a scene that cannot afford the any-hit work (a dense tree in view adds about 2 ms to the lighting passes at 1080p and at 4K). No leaf transmission yet: a leaf lit from the other side stays dark. The reference pathtracer now uses a separate `trace_glass_ray` entry point for camera and BSDF rays, resolving blended hits as smooth thin glass with Fresnel reflection and tinted transmission. Realtime glossy paths also use `trace_glass_ray`, retaining three opaque bounces with a separate 32-pane chain limit. Shadow and diffuse GI rays keep `trace_ray`. See [the glass model and validation](glass.md), including the limits of unattenuated shadow rays.

**Point and spot lights.** Upstream ignores `PointLight` and `SpotLight`; a lamp-lit scene had to fake its lamps as emissive meshes, and the numbers never matched the raster path. Here every point and spot light in the scene is a light source in the ReSTIR DI list, sampled as a sphere of the light's `radius` (floored at 1 cm, since glTF and Bevy default to 0). The units are the raster path's: `bevy_pbr` already turns lumens into candela with its 4π (for spots too), the sphere's surface radiance is that intensity over π r², so a receiver facing the light sees I / d² either way, and Filament's spot cone and range window are applied exactly as `pbr_lighting.wgsl` does. Shadow rays go to the sample point; the sample is a point on the whole sphere (not the cap a receiver would see), because Solari resolves light samples before it knows the receiver (the presampled tiles, and the resampling re-resolves them at other pixels); the far half of the sphere has zero weight and falls out of the resampling without a ray. The sphere is an additive virtual emitter. Ordinary next-event estimation keeps full weight; delta reflection paths and the realtime smooth primary lobe excluded from ReSTIR DI collect ray/sphere hits instead. Directional disks use the same ownership partition. See [analytic reflections](glass.md#analytic-light-reflections). Temporal reuse follows a light across frames by its entity, like a directional light. Diffuse BRDFs differ by design: Solari's Lambert carries a (1 - F) layering term the raster Burley diffuse does not, which is about 0.1 EV on a rough dielectric. The separate local test rig measures this against the pathtracer and a closed-form scene.

**Pathtracer accumulation.** Upstream resets the accumulation whenever `Ref<GlobalTransform>::is_changed()` says the camera moved, and read it from the extract system that fires every frame for a camera that has not moved, so the reference never got past one sample. This fork compares the transform by value. Two diagnostics came with the fix: the pathtracer node logs (at debug level) how many of the last 100 frames reset the accumulation, and `SOLARIK_PATHTRACER_DEBUG_SAMPLE_COUNT` in the environment at startup makes it draw the per-pixel sample count instead of the image as little-endian RGB bytes (`R + 256*G + 65536*B`, capped at 16,777,215). Use an sRGB PNG target with exposure/tonemapping and other postprocessing disabled (`SCENE_LINEAR=1` in the rig). See [the convergence audit](pathtracer-convergence.md).

**Power-weighted light selection.** Each frame the CPU builds a Walker alias table over estimated emitted flux. Emissive meshes use the material's luminance times world-space triangle area times π; point lights use candela times 4π; spots integrate Bevy's squared cone ramp. Directional lights use illuminance times a nominal 1 m² collection area. Emissive textures are not averaged into the estimate. These choices affect variance; each sample is divided by its actual selection probability, and emissive-hit MIS reads that same probability from the instance. The sky remains on the traced miss paths. Mesh edges are cached at upload (24 bytes per triangle) so area follows nonuniform scale and mesh edits; a dark light list falls back to uniform selection.

On the Bistro at frame 140, the 512-frame pathtracer capture is within 0.03 EV of the uniform 8192-frame reference on two cobblestone patches. Against a weighted 8192-frame reference, patch RMSE drops about 36% from uniform selection at the same warmup. Uniform selection already passed the 0.2 EV mean threshold, and the raw realtime output did not measurably improve. These are single-run measurements, not a 16× convergence claim; see [the measurement record](light-sampling.md).

**Emitter MIS measure (development).** Reference, glossy and primary-surface paths compare light and BSDF PDFs in solid angle: `p_omega = p_area * distance² / abs(cos_emitter)`, including the alias-table selection probability. The sampled-light contribution carries a separate `solid_angle_pdf`; its radiance and `inverse_pdf` retain the area formulation used by ReSTIR. The Jacobian uses the world-space triangle cross product, independent of interpolated or normal-mapped shading normals. Straight-through panes and rejected coverage retain the preceding scattering position; delta reflections own subsequent emission.

`cargo test --test emitter_mis -- --ignored --nocapture` executes the production sampling/PDF functions on Vulkan. A 256×256 quadrature over a unit-radiance square emitter gives 0.55412862 against the analytic Lambertian result 0.55412644 at half-width=distance=1; distance=4 gives 0.07347771 against 0.07347764. Scaling both dimensions to 0.1, 4 or 10 preserves radiance, including emitter-selection PMF 0.25. Every sample checks both competing PDFs against an independent CPU formula. Existing glossy glass cases run with and without RR guide code. Bistro realtime/reference captures verify full shader composition; the short reference capture is noisy and is not a convergence measurement. These tests establish the PDF measure correction, not general unbiasedness of the bounded renderer. Backface-emission consistency and shadow transport through glass/coverage remain separate limitations.

Derivation: [PBRT light sampling](https://www.pbr-book.org/3ed-2018/Light_Transport_I_Surface_Reflection/Sampling_Light_Sources) and [emissive-hit MIS](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Light_Transport_I_Surface_Reflection/A_Better_Path_Tracer).

The deferred path is still forced, one emissive mesh may have at most 65,535 triangles, and the scene at most 65,535 light sources of every kind together.
